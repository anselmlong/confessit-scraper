# Product

## Register

product

## Users

NUS students and anyone curious about the NUSConfessIT Telegram channel. Public-facing. Primary job: browse and read confessions — scroll through top posts, read threads, satisfy curiosity about what the NUS student body is actually talking about. Secondary jobs: check trends and channel stats, search for a specific topic or module.

Users arrive casually. They're not on a mission. They're the kind of person who opens the Telegram channel at midnight between study sessions.

## Product Purpose

A public dashboard that surfaces NUSConfessIT data: top posts by time range, all-time stats, full-text search, and individual post pages with replies. Lets anyone explore the channel's history and culture without needing a Telegram account or scrolling through thousands of posts manually.

Success: a visitor lands, immediately sees something interesting, clicks into a post, reads the replies, and comes back the next day.

## Brand Personality

Campus-casual, slightly irreverent, student-built.

Three words: **community-rooted, approachable, unfiltered.**

Tone: warm but not saccharine. Confident but not corporate. The kind of tool a sharp NUS student built for other NUS students — not a startup product, not a university portal. No marketing voice. No feature announcements. Just the data, served well.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboards** (Metabase, Grafana, Datadog): too enterprise, too grey, too many config panels. This is a reading experience, not an ops tool.
- **Social media analytics platforms** (Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Buffer): too marketing-flavored, optimisation-obsessed. We're not helping anyone grow an audience.
- **NUS official web properties** (nus.edu.sg, LumiNUS): institutional, stiff, color-by-committee. The confessions channel exists precisely because students needed somewhere unofficial.

## Design Principles

1. **Content first.** Confessions are the product. The UI is the frame. If a design decision draws attention to itself, it's wrong.
2. **Student-built, not enterprise.** Personality over sterile polish. The tool should feel like someone made it with care, not like it was produced by a committee. Small opinionated choices beat safe defaults.
3. **Browse-native.** The default state is discovery, not search. Reward casual scrolling. Post cards should be skimmable at speed; depth is one tap away.
4. **Honest data.** Stats are context, not performance theater. No inflated metrics, no vanity framing. Show numbers because they're interesting, not because they impress.
5. **Fast enough to be fun.** A sluggish dashboard kills the browsing impulse. Perceived speed is a UX feature, not an engineering afterthought.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Good contrast ratios, keyboard-navigable, screen-reader-friendly labels on all interactive elements and emoji metrics. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
